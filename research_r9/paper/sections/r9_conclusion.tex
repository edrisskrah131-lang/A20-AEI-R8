\section{Conclusion}
An executable task-event interface can delimit reuse of engineering evidence through a requirement containment witness and a trusted invariant dependency projection. Otherwise the service renews applicable evidence or refuses the requested claim. Two independently generated synthetic studies evaluate acquisition, capture-time association and timely reporting while retaining the original numerical solver, competitive fixed policies and adverse route results.

The complete procedural semantic implementation matches the contract, and direct renewal supports more configured requests. Anchor reuse reduces measured cold event re-evaluation but does not establish a graph-exclusive accuracy or runtime advantage. The wider ROI changes acquisition and cost while preserving only a narrow optical projection; full reporting remains unsupported at the primary risk allowance. The release preserves all raw inputs, actual rasters, frozen choices, negative outcomes and code so subsequent engineering validation can extend these stated boundaries.
