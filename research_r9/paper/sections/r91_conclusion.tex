\section{Conclusion}
Executable operating contracts connect aircraft-inspection requirements, anytime localization and the evidence applicable to the pose consumed by a task. The retained service combines an explicit joint state, budgeted registration prefixes, source-version replacement, causal replay and source-scoped evidence lifetime. Its controlled and complete-route experiments establish computation--accuracy--deadline trade-offs while retaining cases in which Fixed2 is preferable. The CART gains are shared by FlatCART, and the complete procedural control reproduces the contract's authorization results.

The additional task interface compiles image-acquisition, capture-time association and report requirements into events. Pointwise containment permits evidence reuse; a producer change permits transfer only through an invariant requested projection. Two further studies show fewer calibration-record evaluations for anchor reuse, shared by the complete procedural implementation, while cached direct renewal supports more configured requests. At allowance 0.15, complete reporting remains unsupported. These results extend the original localization work with auditable evidence semantics and explicit service limits; they do not establish a representation-exclusive numerical gain, a mission certificate or a physical safety guarantee. Improving complete-task feasibility and demonstrating broader engineering value remain the next research steps.
