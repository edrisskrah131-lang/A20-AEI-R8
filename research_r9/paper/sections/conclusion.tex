
\section{Conclusion}
Executable operating contracts connect an inspection requirement to a compatible localization policy, its computational actions and the evidence attached to its result. Multi-action forecasts and source replacement/replay provide the numerical interface. Event cards distinguish trajectory reliability from deadline-pose availability; their intersection selects a policy or refuses a request unsupported by the available catalogue cards. Independent simulation shows more distributed improvement over shallow computation and conditional timing--accuracy tradeoffs. Secondary analysis demonstrates consequential event-driven selection on unfitted geometry, while complete routes retain the competitiveness of fixed shallow registration. The reproducibility release preserves the executable decisions, their empirical benefits and their applicability conditions.
