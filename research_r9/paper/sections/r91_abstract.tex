Aircraft inspection requires timely airframe-relative localization and evidence matched to the consuming task. We develop executable operating contracts linking task requirements, computational budgets, correlated source revisions and population-risk applicability. The retained localization service combines budgeted registration, source replacement, measured-motion replay and source-scoped evidence monitoring. Its prospective study contains 13,824 synthetic episode inputs and 432 complete-route executions. At standard service, the CART policy reduces integrated loss relative to Fixed2 by 15.68\% on nominal geometry and 12.61\% on unfitted geometry, with greater modeled work; FlatCART shares these gains, and Fixed2 remains competitive on ordinary routes. We extend this service to image acquisition, capture-time association and report delivery through requirement-event containment and producer-dependency invariance. Two additional studies execute 12,800 independent synthetic inputs with actual raster processing. At risk allowance 0.15, semantic reuse supports 24/192 and 26/192 configured requests, while cached direct renewal supports 30/192 and 32/192. Anchor preparation evaluates 53.66\% fewer calibration records, with narrower service support. A fully informed procedural implementation matches the contract results, and complete reporting remains uncertified. The contribution is an executable task--source--evidence interface with demonstrated numerical trade-offs, auditable evidence reuse and explicit applicability limits. No new physical localization experiment or universal representation-specific advantage is claimed.
